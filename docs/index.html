what it is

a new drive next to macintosh hd. except the terabytes live somewhere else.

your files look, open and save like normal — in the apps you already use. they just never occupy your disk, and the server only ever holds ciphertext.

01

it mounts like a drive.

Finder on macOS, Explorer on Windows, any path on Linux. POSIX semantics, mmap paths, rename, truncate. video apps and compilers see a filesystem, not a sync folder.

02

it streams, it doesn't copy.

only the byte ranges your app asks for are fetched, decrypted and cached. hit play on a 48 GB film and it starts on the first frames. your disk stays empty.

03

it encrypts before upload.

content-defined chunking, AES-256-GCM per chunk, keys derived from yours via HKDF and bound to fingerprints. the master key never leaves your machine.

security

not even us.

every chunk is sealed on your machine before a byte moves. the provider stores opaque blobs. compromise of one chunk key exposes exactly one chunk.

AES-256-GCM per chunk, unique nonce, AAD-bound to the chunk fingerprint — substitution across chunks fails closed.
HKDF-SHA256 derives every chunk key from your master key; the master never reaches storage or our api.
fastcdc boundaries mean an edit re-encrypts only its neighbourhood — not the whole file, not the tail.
the index stays local. filenames, chunk maps and tags sit in an encrypted-at-rest sqlite file on your device.

questions, answered

the obvious ones.

what is lumen?
a drive that mounts next to macintosh hd with terabytes behind it. files open in place, take zero bytes on disk, and stay end-to-end encrypted. an mcp server exposes the same drive to agents.
does it work on my mac?
macOS 12 monterey or newer, intel or apple silicon — one universal build. we deliberately don't use apple's 26-only drive api, so older macs are first-class instead of abandoned.
windows and linux?
yes. windows uses winfsp, linux uses fuse3, both over the same rust core and mount semantics. the windows zip ships from the same tag as the mac dmg.
how is this different from icloud, dropbox or google drive?
they sync or hydrate whole files and fill your disk. lumen is a filesystem: apps request byte ranges, the client fetches and decrypts just those. a 48 GB film never occupies 48 GB to play.
how is it different from helumi?
helumi is mac-only, requires macOS 26.2, locks storage to cloudflare r2 and flat-charges $10. lumen runs on 12+, windows and linux, takes any s3 bucket, costs $3 plus cost, and speaks MCP natively instead of just appearing in finder.
will it work with davinci, final cut, premiere, photoshop?
they see a normal drive, so yes. the test suite pins xcode build-in-volume, docker bind-mounts and video scrub patterns specifically because those are where fake drives break.
where are my files stored?
in the bucket you choose — r2, b2, storj, minio or your own s3 endpoint. managed mode routes to the cheapest provider per file. everything arrives there already encrypted.
what if i lose my key?
a recovery key is generated at setup. keep it. with it you're back in; without it nobody opens the data, including us.
does it fill up my disk?
no. default residency is zero bytes. pin a folder for offline and only that folder occupies space; unpin and the space returns.
can i work offline?
pinned folders, yes. new writes queue locally and upload when the network returns, resuming mid-chunk after drops.
what happens if i stop paying?
nothing is deleted. reads and mounts keep working for 30 days; writes resume when you pick a plan again.
refunds?
full refund within 3 days of the first payment, once per person. renewals aren't refunded; cancel any time.
what does beta mean here?
the core, crypto and billing paths are built and tested end to end (110 tests, zero clippy warnings). expect sharp edges in installer polish, not in data safety. changelog is public.
